TUM · RL workshop · Apple scenario

Q-learning for weekly portfolio allocation

From the grid-world Bellman update to a portfolio decision: each week the agent chooses how much to hold in Apple stock versus cash. It sees short- and long-horizon momentum, and learns from returns, transaction costs, and an optional downside penalty.

Explore the code and notebooksDownload Apple data

Educational demonstration, not a trading strategy. The data cover just 2018–2019 and are insufficient to validate predictive investment performance.

1

The weekly allocation decision

Changing exposure may be valuable when conditions change; each adjustment can incur a cost.

01

What do we control?

Our portfolio weight: how much capital to allocate to Apple stock versus cash at each weekly decision.

02

What is uncertain?

Future returns. We observe recent price momentum, but neither the next return nor the true market state is known.

03

Why is it sequential?

Moving from 0% to 100% exposure has a cost. Today's weight becomes the starting point for tomorrow's decision.

01 · Problem formulation

The investment decision as an MDP

The action changes the exposure and the cost of the next adjustment. The market return itself is unaffected by this price-taking investor.

Observe a state

12 possible situations

4-week momentum: up / down
12-week momentum: up / down
Previous Apple target: 0 / 50 / 100%

2 × 2 × 3 = 12 states
Choose an action

Three portfolio weights

0%Cash
50%Split
100%Apple

All remaining capital is held in cash, assumed to earn zero interest.

Learn from the result

A reward, then a new state

After one week, the agent observes the realized return and updates a Q-value using a Bellman-based temporal-difference target.

Q(s,a) ← Q(s,a) + α [r + γ max Q(s′,·) − Q(s,a)]
Reward (decimal units)
r = w × Rnext − c × |w − wprevious| − λ × max(−w × Rnext, 0)

c is a simplified transaction-cost rate; λ is a downside penalty used only in the learning objective. It is not deducted from realized net portfolio wealth. The 50% target is treated as maintained between decisions; the toy cost model charges for changes in target weights, not small within-week drift corrections.

    04 · Discussion

    Interpretation and limitations

    As in the grid-world demo, Q-values are learned from transitions. Unlike grid world, the investor cannot run truly independent experiments on past markets.

    Why two signals?

    The second horizon creates more possible market states: 12 instead of 6. It can capture signal disagreements, but increases sparsity given only 64 distinct training decisions.

    Why account for trading costs?

    Today’s target weight affects the cost of tomorrow’s adjustment. This creates a sequential decision problem, rather than just predicting next week’s return.

    Why benchmark classical alternatives?

    RL must be compared with transparent decision rules or suitable dynamic optimization baselines before we can say its added complexity is useful.

    Why not jump to PPO?

    With 105 weekly price points, a neural policy is harder to audit and potentially easier to overfit. The notebook demonstrates learning mechanics, not production-ready portfolio management.

    Data: Plotly demonstration weekly normalized stock prices (AAPL, 2018–2019; dividend adjustment unverified). Motivation: Bernd Scherer (2020), Maschinelles Lernen im Asset Management, Absolut|report, pp. 46–51. These sources do not establish RL outperformance.       01 · Environment design

      Two risky assets, six choices and a larger state space

      At each weekly decision, the agent sees whether SPY and TLT rose over the previous four weeks and remembers its last allocation. Its action chooses new SPY and TLT weights; leftover wealth stays in zero-yield cash.

      ACTION

      REWARD

      Net weekly reward

      Portfolio return after trading costs minus the fixed 50/50 SPY–TLT reference return and a downside penalty. Zero cash yield. The reward is not a compounded wealth return.

        A look ahead

        What if we observed many more signals?

        Two momentum signs and the previous choice make 24 states here. Adding many continuous indicators quickly makes a Q-table hard to fill from experience. A deep Q-network can approximate action values with a neural network. That is a preview of a later topic, not the model running in this workshop.
